1. PURPOSE AND NON-NEGOTIABLE DESIGN PRINCIPLES

The four tracks are considered finalized. Participants’ submitted presentations are indicators of
interests and technical directions; they are not finalized challenge statements and should not be
copied as such. The purpose of this brief is to translate those interests into problems
recognizable as useful, consequential problem statements by professionals in relevant industrial
domains, viz. logistics, energy, defense, finance, pharmaceuticals, health systems, security,
agriculture and industrial R&D.

Problem statements and associated deliverables are open to interpretation and are intended to
provide a foundational direction rather than impose rigid constraints. Participants are
encouraged to exercise creative freedom and explore innovative approaches, provided their
solutions preserve and build upon the core intent and objectives outlined. Participant’s chosen
problem statement and their solution must have: (1) a suitably attributable industrial domain; (2)
an observable business or scientific outcome; (3) a mathematically valid quantum/optimization
or track-specific technical component; and (4) an end-to-end implementation without replacing
the real problem with an unrelated toy.

There are five problem statements per track: Quantum Machine Learning (QML), Post-Quantum
Cryptography (PQC), Web3 & Blockchain, and Generative AI & Machine Learning. Teams
should select freely within their confirmed track.

4. TRACK I: QUANTUM MACHINE LEARNING

Mandatory for ALL five: QpiAI QCloud Quantum + QpiAI Opt QUBO / Ising / HUBO optimization.

QML-01: Delay-Risk-Aware Fleet Dispatch for Contract Logistics

Problem. A contract logistics operator has finite vehicles, vehicle-specific capacities, delivery
windows and contractual commitments. Traffic and service-time uncertainty can make a
cheap-looking route fail its SLA. Produce vehicle routes that are operationally feasible and
minimize actual transport/labor expense together with estimated service-failure exposure.

Deliver. Exact vehicle-to-route plan, ordered stops, ETA/risk for each service commitment and
total financial exposure. Evaluate SLA violations, valid coverage, cost, prediction calibration,
solver quality and runtime.
QML-02: Grid-Constrained Commercial EV Fleet Charging

Problem. Vehicle charging must finish before real departure deadlines without exceeding
feeder/transformer limits or driving expensive power peaks. Select charger-and-time allocations
minimizing energy tariff costs and demand charges while meeting all confirmed departure
requirements.

Deliver. Vehicle/charger/time-slot plan, energy delivered, predicted departures, aggregate kW
loading and bill. Measure departure compliance, peak kW, tariff cost and capacity violations.

QML-03: Retired Battery Qualification and Second-Life Allocation

Problem. Retired batteries differ in health, electrical properties and degradation. Safely decide
which units qualify for reuse, grouping or recycling while protecting minimum compatibility and
value constraints.

Deliver. Unit-by-unit reuse/testing/recycling disposition, grouped capacity, compatibility rationale
and uncertainty. Evaluate health estimation error, forbidden-assignment count, value, pack
mismatch.

QML-04: Assay-Constrained Selection of Drug-Discovery Compounds

Problem. A drug-discovery team can test only a limited number of molecules and must trade
predicted activity against chemical novelty, diversity, assay expense and availability. Select a
high-value next experimental batch for one specified target and assay.

Deliver. Specific molecules, source IDs, cost, predicted measurements/uncertainty, scaffold
diversity and validation plan. Evaluate retrospective enrichment on held-out assay results, novel
scaffold coverage and selection value.

QML-05: Discrete Foreign-Exchange Hedge Allocation

Problem. Dated contractual foreign-currency cashflows expose a company to adverse FX
moves, but forwards/options are purchasable only in discrete tradable lots and constrained by
approved risk policy, credit and liquidity. Select economically appropriate hedge lots and dates.

Deliver. Hedge tickets, quantities, maturities, quoted costs and residual scenario exposures
with compliance report. Benchmark held-out risk and implementation cost.
5. TRACK II: POST-QUANTUM CRYPTOGRAPHY

PQC-01: Verification-Gated Cryptographic Migration Across Banking
Applications

Problem. A bank has vulnerable RSA/ECC-dependent services, certificates and software
dependencies that cannot all transition simultaneously without interrupting payment services.
Identify current cryptographic exposure and prioritize a provably compatible migration schedule.

Deliver. Inventory with file/service evidence, migration sequence, working before-and-after
protocol traces, compatibility tests and rollback plan. Evaluate detection precision/recall on
labelled code and configs, protocol correctness, overhead and feasible migration dependencies.

PQC-02: Post-Quantum Authentication of Automotive Firmware Updates

Problem. Software-defined vehicles receive firmware updates on constrained ECUs.
Forged/rolled-back firmware must be rejected, while real signature sizes, verification time and
recovery constraints have to remain acceptable over long platform lives.

Deliver. Runnable signer and verifier, signed update manifest, measured signature
bytes/latency/RAM/flash, failure-recovery results and negative-test suite. Hardware profiles must
be measured on an identified available device.

PQC-03: Quantum-Resistant Satellite Command Authentication

Problem. Satellites accept high-consequence commands over limited-bandwidth
communications, often with low repairability. Unauthorized or replayed instructions must be
rejected without exhausting onboard computation or link budgets.

Deliver. Ground-side signing, onboard-side receiving verification, accepted/rejected log and
measured resource budget. Test tampered, replayed, expired, unauthorized and forged
commands.

PQC-04: Long-Term Confidentiality in Hospital-to-Laboratory Data
Exchange

Problem. Medical reports are retained for long periods; captured encrypted traffic may face
future quantum threats. Migration to new public-key mechanisms must protect confidential
transfers without breaking interoperability.
Deliver. Complete secure record exchange, validated protocol handshake, negative-case tests
and an explicit key-management/compatibility report. Evaluate session establishment
correctness, authorization rejection, data integrity and end-to-end latency.

PQC-05: PQC Deployment in Resource-Constrained Industrial Gateways

Problem. Industrial gateways may remain operational for a decade or more under severe
processor, memory, frame-size and downtime constraints. Cryptographic migration must
maintain authenticated communication and configuration updates without interrupting control
functions.

Deliver. Working secure gateway session, signed-update verification, hardware/resource
measurements and deployment plan. Distinguish device measurements from measurements on
a desktop emulator.

6. TRACK III: WEB3 & BLOCKCHAIN

WEB3-01: Agricultural Procurement, Quality Grading and Payment
Settlement

Problem. Agricultural lot value depends on verified delivered weight, quality grading, loss,
transfer and contract terms. Unverifiable deductions and duplicated claims create disputes
between growers, collectors, warehouses and processors.

Deliver. Full lot ancestry, mass/quantity reconciliation, attested grading, settlement records,
dispute evidence and feasible commercial allocation. Evaluate false/duplicate settlement
rejection, quantity consistency, contractual fulfillment and cost.

WEB3-02: E-Waste Collection, Recovery Yields and Auditable Material
Claims

Problem. E-waste collected from disparate channels can be misreported, lost between
handovers or counted twice toward material recovery. An industrial recycler needs traceable
custody and reconcilable physical yield evidence.

Deliver. Traceable batch graph, processor evidence, validated mass-balance, duplicate-claim
checks and dispatch assignment. Evaluate independent inventory reconciliation, missing
handovers, cost and licensed-facility compliance.
WEB3-03: Cross-Enterprise Digital Passports for EV Battery Reuse and
Recycling

Problem. Batteries change ownership and use across manufacturers, repairers,
remanufacturers and recyclers. Every party needs authenticated identity and condition history
but should only access information authorized for its role.

Deliver. Verified battery-lifecycle state, independently inspectable custody/history evidence,
access-control tests and disposition decisions. Measure unauthorized write rejection, lineage
continuity, missing attestation detection and allocation feasibility.

WEB3-04: Rights-Gated Regional-Language Dataset Licensing for AI
Training

Problem. AI model training datasets may carry different rights for commercial training,
geography, derivative works, redistribution, expiry or revocation. Unauthorized data ingestion
creates substantial contractual and reputational exposure.

Deliver. Signed rights ledger, ingestion decision API, accepted/rejected corpus manifests, audit
log, licensing-cost allocation and adversarial policy checks. Evaluate false authorizations,
correct denial behavior, traceability and coverage.

WEB3-05: Policy-Bound Autonomous Procurement and
Machine-to-Machine Payments

Problem. Autonomous software can buy low-value supplies/services efficiently but must not
exceed delegated authority, buy from unauthorized vendors or obey malicious instructions that
manipulate payment policy.

Deliver. Agent request, approved purchase basket, signed authority, executable payment on a
controlled test environment, transaction proofs and revocation. Evaluate unauthorized-payment
rejection, purchasing costs, budget enforcement and verifier failure handling.

7. TRACK IV: GENERATIVE AI & MACHINE LEARNING

GENAI-01: Freight Exception Resolution Before Demurrage and Delivery
Penalties

Problem. Documentation, customs release, terminal deadlines and truck bookings are
fragmented across carrier emails, shipping documents, status feeds and internal notes. Missing
an action can cause avoidable demurrage, detention, storage or missed deliveries.
Deliver. Container-level blocker, verified evidence, time-bound responsible action, feasible
truck/slot allocation and projected avoidable costs. Assess evidence precision, missed blockers,
false escalations, deadline compliance and historical expense.

GENAI-02: Security Incident Triage and Qualified Analyst Assignment

Problem. SOC analysts must group related identity, endpoint and cloud alerts, preserve
evidential context and assign urgent cases to qualified responders under capacity and
response-time contracts.

Deliver. Auditable incident queue, links to contributing logs, assigned specialists, response
plans and uncertainty. Evaluate evidence accuracy, grouping precision, important incident recall,
response SLA adherence and analyst workload.

GENAI-03: Bus Headway Recovery and Passenger Disruption Updates

Problem. Traffic and uneven boarding cause buses to bunch. Dispatchers choose whether to
hold, short-turn, release a spare or make no change without breaking driver, stop-coverage and
fleet constraints.

Deliver. Specific dispatcher hold/short-turn/spare orders, predicted revised arrivals and
accurate bilingual or multilingual passenger information where relevant. Evaluate predicted ETA
error, headway regularity, missed service, feasibility and notification truthfulness.

GENAI-04: Expiry-Aware Essential Medicine Redistribution Across Health
Facilities

Problem. Some medical facilities stock out of essential drugs while nearby facilities hold eligible
lots close to expiry. Redistribution is constrained by actual inventories, drug identity, minimum
stock reserve, shelf life, permitted transport and cold chain.

Deliver. Auditable lot-level transfer orders, quantities, source/destination, temperature
requirements, shortage reduction and expected expiry avoidance. Evaluate actual stockout
reductions, expiry and inventory feasibility.

GENAI-05: Plant-Disease Investigation Coupled to Real Agronomy-Service
Capacity

Problem. Visually similar crop damage may arise from pests, disease, nutrient stress or
weather. Field managers need a verified diagnosis pathway and realistic scheduling of
agronomists, testing services and approved supplies; generic crop-image diagnosis is
insufficient.

Deliver. Auditable per-field investigation plan, validated next test, appointment, available
permitted inputs (when independently confirmed) and service orders. Evaluate diagnosis quality
on independent field observations, unsafe advice, turnaround and feasibility.
